📖 How it Works: Educational Guide for Beginners (Click to expand)

1. Tokenization

Computers cannot read letters or words directly. A tokenizer converts text into numeric IDs using a vocabulary. If a full word isn't recognized, subword rules split it into pieces (like "brave" + "ly").

2. Word Embeddings

Each token ID maps to a list of numbers called a vector. Because random numbers carry no meaning, embeddings place semantically related words (like "king" and "queen") close to each other in vector space.

3. Positional Encoding

Unlike humans who read left-to-right, transformers process an entire sentence at once in parallel. Sinusoidal frequency waves are added so the network knows whether "dog" was the 1st or 6th word.

4. Cosine Similarity & Softmax

Cosine similarity measures the angle between two word vectors. Softmax converts these raw scores into positive percentages that sum to 100%, allowing the model to sample the most likely next word.
